## What You MAY Do

1. **Use the name to refer to the project** — e.g., "powered by Translify", "a
   Translify plugin", "compatible with Translify".

2. **Fork and modify the code** under the MIT License — as long as you rename
   your fork to avoid confusion with the official Translify project.

3. **Write about, teach, or blog about Translify** — including the name in
   articles, tutorials, and videos is explicitly allowed.

4. **Create plugins and integrations** — naming your package things like
   `translify-plugin-vue` or `translify-provider-deepl` is acceptable.

## What You MAY NOT Do

1. **Republish this package under the same or similar name** — you may not
   publish a package named `translify`, `@*/translify`, or any confusingly
   similar variant (e.g., `translify-cli`, `translify-io`, `translify-official`)
   to npm, JSR, or any other registry without permission.

2. **Rename and rebrand** — you may not take the Translify source code and
   redistribute it under the "Translify" name as if it were your own product.

3. **Use the logo without permission** — the Translify logo may not be used in
   products, marketing materials, or merchandise without written permission.

4. **Imply official affiliation** — you may not use the trademark in a way that
   implies official endorsement by the Translify project unless you have
   explicit permission.

## Summary

Put simply: use the code freely, but don't pretend your fork is the official
Translify or publish it under the Translify name. Rename your fork. This
protects the reputation and quality signal of the official project while keeping
the software free and open.
